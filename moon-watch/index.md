## How the numbers are made

Expected flashes per hour = detection efficiency x [sum over active showers of k_shower x activity profile x observable fraction + sporadic constant x dark visible area]. The observable fraction is the share of a shower's lunar impacts that land on the Earth-facing night side (cos chi weighting with the horizon cut, Madiedo et al. 2015 eq. 4), from the Moon's real position, phase and libration. Efficiency is 1 for the NASA 0.35 m program that sets the units; every other observing program (MIDAS, UEC, NELIOTA, TILT, LUMIO) has its own fitted efficiency, shrunk toward an aperture law (aperture/0.35 m)^1.5, and the public scope classes (30-40 cm, 20 cm, 10 cm) carry a class efficiency with its own uncertainty (20 cm and 10 cm are aperture extrapolations; 10 cm is flagged). k for the Geminids, Taurids (northern branch tied to the southern), Lyrids and Perseids and the sporadic constant are fitted to 30 published sessions; every other shower is provisional (scaled from the Geminids, x3 band). Bands are 90 % model bands (log-normal, one sigma for every term, estimated out of sample by leaving each observing program out in turn and floored at 0.5); a count over N hours is further Poisson-scattered. Rates assume your field covers the dark side as the calibration programs' did. Not fully validated.

Scope classes (efficiency relative to the NASA 0.35 m program; sigma = the class's efficiency uncertainty):

- 30-40cm (aperture 0.35 m): efficiency 0.97, sigma 0.03
- 20cm (aperture 0.20 m): efficiency 0.42, sigma 0.67
- 10cm (aperture 0.10 m): efficiency 0.15, sigma 0.67, extrapolated

Per-city hours for any window are in `moon-watch.json`; any site: `python -m moonwatch.forecast --site lat,lon,Zone/Name`.
